Schengen visa refusal in Indonesia: appeal or reapply

Short answer. A refusal comes on one standard European form with seventeen numbered grounds, and that same form carries the appeal authority and the deadline — usually 30 days to two months. There is no cooling-off period: you may reapply the next day, but only if you have fixed the reason. Appeal when the consulate got a fact wrong; otherwise a fresh application with real evidence of your ties to Indonesia is the stronger move.

Updated: 5 November 2026 · Verified by the Malina Visa team

Start with the refusal letter itself

A Schengen refusal does not come as a free-form letter. It comes on one standard European form, set out in Annex VI to the EU Visa Code (Regulation 810/2009), with seventeen numbered grounds and a tick box next to each. The consul ticks one or several. That number is your reason — not «they didn't like something».

The same form obliges the consulate to fill in four more fields: the national law under which you may appeal, the authority that hears it, where to read the procedure, and — on its own line — the deadline. So both the address and the time limit are already printed on your refusal. There is no point hunting for them online: every country has its own, and advice from a chat group is probably about a different one.

One exception. Ground 17 — a visa annulled at the holder's own request — is the only one that cannot be appealed. The other sixteen can. The decision must be handed to you in the language of the deciding country plus one more official EU language; that is Article 32 of the Code.

What the common grounds actually mean

The wording on the form is legal and terse. Here is how it reads in practice — especially for someone applying on a KITAS from Indonesia rather than from their home country.

GroundOn the formWhat it means for you
2Purpose and conditions of stay not justifiedThe itinerary doesn't match the bookings; it isn't clear why there, why those dates
3Means of subsistence not provenShort statements, irregular income, balance that doesn't cover the trip
11Reasonable doubts about the statements madeThe form contradicts the documents: job, income, address, family
12Doubts about the authenticity of documentsA letter with no signature or contacts, an uncertified translation, a «made» booking
13Doubts about your intention to leave before the visa expiresThe most common one: nothing shows you have somewhere to come back to
16No valid travel medical insuranceCover below EUR 30,000, or not for the whole stay and the whole territory

Ground 13 is not the consul being difficult. Article 21 of the Code explicitly requires them to assess migration risk and your intention to leave. So what you have to prove is not that you are a decent person — it is your tie to the place where you live.

Appeals: every country, its own deadline

The right to appeal sits in Article 32(3): you appeal against the country that took the final decision, under that country's law. The deadlines are short and they differ.

CountryWhereDeadline
FranceDeputy director for visas at the Interior Ministry (short-stay visas since 2023 — no longer the CRRV)30 days; the administrative appeal is compulsory first
GermanyCourt, or a fresh applicationRemonstration no longer exists
NetherlandsObjection (bezwaar) to the IND4 weeks

SwitzerlandState Secretariat for Migration, Bern30 days + CHF 200 paid in advance

The change most guides have missed. Germany abolished remonstration — the written objection you used to send to the embassy — worldwide on 1 July 2025. German missions no longer accept, review or answer those letters. If someone tells you to «write a remonstration», that advice is eighteen months out of date: after a German refusal you are left with the Berlin Administrative Court or a new application.

Appeal, or simply apply again

There is no formal cooling-off period after a refusal — not in the Visa Code, not in national rules. The German Federal Foreign Office says it plainly: a new application may be submitted at any time. The Dutch government openly offers a fresh application as the faster alternative to an objection, while warning that the fee is payable again and is not refunded.

  • ✓Appeal when the consulate got a fact wrong: missed a document you filed, misread the dates, confused you with a namesake
  • ✓Reapply when the reason is real and can be closed with paperwork: better statements, proper insurance, a cleaner itinerary
  • ✓Change the country only if the trip itself changed. The same itinerary filed at a different consulate is a second refusal waiting to happen

Where to apply if you live in Bali

Two rules decide everything. Article 5 picks the country: the one where you will spend most days, or where the main purpose lies; first entry is only the fallback. Article 6 picks the place: the consulate of that country in the jurisdiction where you legally reside.

Which means your right to apply in Indonesia comes from the KITAS. On a short-stay visa you will be sent back to your country of residence — the Swiss embassy in Jakarta states this in so many words. And one requirement people trip over: the KITAS must stay valid for at least three months after you return to Indonesia, not after you apply. Both the German embassy and the Greek visa centre confirm it.

Decisions are taken in Jakarta only: in the European Commission's 2025 statistics all nineteen Schengen consulates processing applications in Indonesia sit there. Documents and biometrics, however, can be submitted in Bali — officially confirmed by Germany (21 days of processing against 15 in Jakarta) and by the Netherlands, through the VFS centre in Tuban. France and Spain have no Bali centre. Fingerprints are not retaken if you gave them for a Schengen visa within the past 59 months. How the application works on a KITAS is covered in our guide to applying for Schengen from Indonesia.

How often refusals actually happen

European Commission figures for 2025: 223,862 applications filed in Indonesia, 11,504 not issued — 5.2%, up from 4.28% a year earlier. The world average is 14.6%, almost three times higher. The spread inside Jakarta matters more: Croatia refused 9.9% of applicants, Switzerland 9.2%, the Netherlands 7.9% — against 2.1% for Czechia, 2.6% for France on the largest flow of 66,000 applications, and 2.7% for Spain. A fourfold difference under one and the same Visa Code.

What to do with that. Not «apply where they are softer» — the main-destination rule still stands, and filing with the wrong country is itself a ground for refusal. But when an honest itinerary genuinely allows two main destinations, the statistics are a fair tie-breaker.

How to make the second attempt stronger

The list of evidence for your intention to return is written into Annex II of the Code, and for an expat it reads surprisingly well: funds are proven in the country of residence, not of citizenship. So an Indonesian bank, an Indonesian contract and a KITAS are not «extra paperwork» — they are exactly what the law names.

1. Ties to Indonesia

KITAS, rental agreement, three months of Indonesian bank statements, employment contract or your PT PMA documents, children's school.

2. Money without questions

Original statements for three months, regular credits, a balance that covers every day of the trip. A single transfer the day before you file works against you.

3. An itinerary that adds up

Accommodation booked for every night, flight dates matching the bookings, main country = the one with most days.

4. Insurance to spec

From EUR 30,000, whole stay, whole territory. That is ground 16 — the easiest one to remove.

5. A cover letter

Who you are, where you live, why you are going and why you will come back. Optional, but it answers half of grounds 2 and 13.

Passport requirements are worth rechecking before you file — they are collected in our document rules guide.

EES and ETIAS: what is live and what is not

The Entry/Exit System went live on 12 October 2025 and has covered every external border since 10 April 2026. It replaced the passport stamp with an electronic record: fingerprints, facial image, visa data, crossing dates. It applies to Schengen visa C holders too, not only to visa-free travellers. The practical effect for you: overstaying the 90/180 rule is now visible automatically — and that is ground 5 on the refusal form.

ETIAS is not running. The Commission's own page says the system is not in operation and applications are not accepted, with the start date to be announced in advance and a fee of EUR 20. More importantly, ETIAS is an authorisation for nationals of visa-free countries. If you need a Schengen visa, it does not replace one.

FAQ

How long must I wait before reapplying?

You don't. Neither the Visa Code nor national rules impose a waiting period — the German Foreign Office states that a new application may be submitted at any time. But reapplying without fixing the reason almost always produces a second refusal.

Can I still send a remonstration to Germany?

No. Germany abolished remonstration worldwide on 1 July 2025; missions no longer accept or answer those letters. What remains is the Berlin Administrative Court or a new application.

Where do I find the appeal deadline?

On the refusal itself. The Annex VI form obliges the consulate to print the appeal authority and the time limit. France allows 30 days, the Netherlands 4 weeks, Italy 60 days, Switzerland 30 days plus CHF 200 in advance.

Do I have to fly to Jakarta?

Decisions are taken in Jakarta — all nineteen consulates are there. But documents and biometrics for Germany and the Netherlands can be submitted in Bali. Fingerprints are not retaken if you gave them for a Schengen visa within the past 59 months.

How much KITAS validity do I need?

At least three months beyond your return to Indonesia, not beyond the application date. The German embassy in Jakarta and the Greek visa centre both confirm it. On a short-stay visa you cannot apply in Indonesia at all.
